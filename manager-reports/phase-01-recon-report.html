Phase 1 of the project · Research

What it takes to ace the CCC in Python

We looked into the Canadian Computing Competition (CCC) and worked out everything a complete beginner needs to learn, in order, to reach a top score on the Senior contest using Python.

Completed 21 September 2026 · Checked against the contest organiser's own website, not memory

What this phase did

  • Studied the contest's current format and rules.
  • Went through every past contest problem from 2014 to 2026 (over 100 problems).
  • Turned that into a step-by-step learning plan of 105 lessons. Each lesson is linked to the real past problems that use it.
  • Compared tools that help keep the future learning app looking polished, and picked one to recommend.

Nothing was installed and no app work has started.

The contest at a glance

5problems, from easy to very hard
75marks in total (15 each)
3 honline, supervised at school
0perfect scores in 2026
  • Students can resubmit answers; only their best attempt counts, and wrong tries cost nothing.
  • Part marks are available for solving the easier parts of each problem.
  • AI tools and pre-written code are banned, so students must know the techniques by heart.
  • Python is allowed, but it runs an older version of the language. The course will teach only what that version supports, plus ways to make programs fast enough.

How hard is a top score?

Very hard, and getting harder. Nobody in Canada scored 75 out of 75 in 2026; the best was 69. The organiser itself says a perfect score may not always be possible in Python, because a few of the hardest problems need more speed than Python can give.

Needs your approval

Two decisions before we continue

1. Design tool for the app

Approve or choose backup

We recommend Impeccable, a popular free design helper. Why: it is the only option that keeps all of the app's many lesson pages looking consistent, and it checks readability automatically.

Upside

  • Consistent, polished look across dozens of pages
  • Built-in checks for readability and accessibility
  • Actively maintained, widely used

Downside

  • Made by an outside developer, not Anthropic
  • Heavier: downloads extra software and runs automatic checks
  • Changes often, so we would lock a version

Backup: Anthropic's own "frontend-design" tool. Simpler, no outside software, but does less to keep pages consistent. Choose it if you prefer no third-party code.

2. Score target

Confirm framing

The course teaches everything needed for 75/75, but tells learners honestly that about 63–68 is the realistic best in Python.

Upside

  • Honest, sets fair expectations
  • Still covers the full perfect-score material

Downside

  • Might feel less ambitious to some learners
  • The 63–68 figure is our estimate, not an official number

Known gaps

  • Python speed on the hardest problems is partly estimated. A key practice website blocked our research, so we could not confirm every case.
  • The 2026 official solutions are not yet published, so the newest hardest problem is not fully understood.
  • Score cut-offs for awards and the national round are estimates; the organiser does not publish them.
  • The contest's Python version could be upgraded for 2027. We will re-check before the app launches.

What comes next

  1. You approve (or change) the two decisions above.
  2. We start writing the actual lessons, following the 105-lesson plan.
  3. The design tool is installed only after your approval. Building the app itself waits for a later go-ahead.
